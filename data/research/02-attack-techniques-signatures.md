# ORCA-NTN synthetic attack-signature report

## Summary

The data card provides useful observations for detecting **radio-link degradation**, **management activity**, **configuration changes**, **service demand**, **gateway conditions**, and **power events**. Its strongest detection opportunities are:

- **RF interference:** noise floor, SINR, BER, capacity, throughput, latency, packet loss and gateway carrier metrics.
- **Compromised management access:** unusual VPN or management sessions, command bursts, large target counts and configuration drift.
- **Terminal disruption:** many devices becoming unavailable or disappearing from telemetry.
- **Fault-versus-attack separation:** compare link degradation to expected rain fade and weather, check the sun-outage flag, and correlate anomalies with authorized maintenance and power conditions.

Several requested signatures **cannot be observed directly** from the current tables. There are no GNSS receiver measurements, packet or signalling records, authentication outcome details, per-terminal management records, or measurements of traffic confidentiality. In particular, the data card cannot identify GNSS spoofing or SS7/Diameter abuse directly. Add the fields listed under **New data-card fields** before treating these as learnable detector classes.

### Incidents and evidence limits

- **KA-SAT/AcidRain:** Viasat reported a ground-based intrusion through a misconfigured VPN appliance into a trusted management segment, lateral movement to the network operations segment, and use of legitimate commands to affect many modems. The report documents high-volume traffic beginning around 03:02 UTC on 24 February 2022, followed by a gradual modem decline and a larger loss of modems beginning around 04:15 UTC. It does **not** publish the initial access time or total pre-impact dwell. [Viasat incident overview](https://www.viasat.com/perspectives/corporate/2022/ka-sat-network-cyber-attack-overview/)
- **GNSS jamming and spoofing:** EASA describes both as continuing operational issues, including affected regions and reported aviation impacts. This is evidence of real interference behavior, but it does not establish that the ORCA-NTN telemetry can detect it. [EASA GNSS outages and alterations](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/air-operations/global-navigation-satellite-system-outages-and-alterations)
- **Satellite interference:** ITU materials document harmful satellite interference and distinguish uplink interference considerations. SPARTA defines jamming as RF energy used to deny communications, navigation or timing signals. [ITU statement on satellite jamming](https://www.itu.int/net/pressoffice/press_releases/2012/OS01.aspx), [ITU-R S.2409](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/rep/R-REP-S.2409-2017-PDF-E.pdf), [SPARTA EX-0016](https://sparta.aerospace.org/technique/EX-0016/)
- **VSAT and ground-segment security:** The peer-reviewed VSAsTer study reports a systematic assessment of VSAT command-and-control practices and identifies security weaknesses. It does **not** establish that all GEO user traffic is unencrypted. A specific, citable study substantiating a broad claim of “unencrypted GEO traffic” was **NOT FOUND** in this research. [VSAsTer, ACM WiSec 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3643833.3656139)
- **Telecom signalling:** The FCC documents SS7 and Diameter vulnerabilities and the risk of subscriber location tracking. The 2026 ITU-T Q.3066 recommendation discusses detection approaches for signalling attacks. The data card has no signalling-plane fields to represent those events. [FCC Public Notice DA 24-308](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-24-308A1.pdf), [ITU-T Q.3066](https://www.itu.int/epublications/publication/itu-t-q-3066-2026-01-principles-for-detection-and-mitigation-of-signalling-attacks-in-telecommunication-networks)

## False-positive-twin table

| Candidate attack signature | Benign twin | Useful separation evidence in this dataset | Remaining ambiguity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Higher noise floor, lower SINR, higher BER, and worse throughput | **Monsoon rain fade** | Compare `fade_residual_dB` to `expected_fade_dB` and correlate by site/zone with `rain_rate_mm_h`, humidity and weather. A weather-consistent fade should track the expected model more closely. | Expected fade is a model feature; the accuracy and calibration range in Chennai weather are not specified in the card. Treat residual thresholds as **ASSUMPTION**. |
| Satellite link degradation around predictable periods | **Sun outage** | Use gateway `sun_outage_flag` and expected timing if available. | Flag definition, precision and coverage are **NOT FOUND** in the card. |
| RF/link impairment across a microwave path | **Rain fade, equipment fault, or path obstruction** | Compare weather, `expected_fade_dB`, power telemetry, `active_path`, and whether satellite gateway metrics show a corresponding impairment. | Current card lacks per-radio alarm codes and path-specific diagnostics. |
| Sudden increase in service demand or packet loss | **Deepavali, Pongal, payday, Chepauk match or event-cell surge** | Check `event_flag`, `active_users`, transaction/call rates, and whether demand rises alongside throughput rather than unexplained management events. | No packet rates, per-service QoS or queue depth; a true overload and benign surge may look similar at the 1-minute cadence. |
| Management login or configuration changes | **Authorized maintenance window** | Compare user, source category, endpoint, login hour, command types, target count and config drift with an explicit approved-change record. | No change-ticket ID, approval identity, MFA result or maintenance-window field is currently defined. |
| Many sites become unavailable | **Fibre cut, gateway fault, grid loss, genset/battery issue** | Check `active_path`, site and gateway clustering, `grid_on`, `genset_on`, `battery_soc_pct`, `hours_left`, and weather/flood conditions. | Current labels have broad fault classes; detailed failure cause codes and topology dependency records would improve attribution. |
| Repeated low-quality or failed traffic | **Legitimate signalling burst or subscriber/event surge** | Correlate with user and service demand, zone, event, and time of day. | SS7/Diameter/5G signalling records are absent, so protocol-level abuse cannot be inferred. |
| Abrupt terminal disappearance after a management command | **Power failure or planned reboot** | Compare management events with site power telemetry and authorized work records; check whether loss is clustered by command target list. | Per-terminal power and per-terminal command results are absent; site-level power may not explain individual terminal outages. |

## Attack-stage timelines

These timelines distinguish **reported incident timing** from **synthetic staging assumptions**. Public reports often document the disruption more clearly than the preceding access and dwell.

| Stage | Publicly documented timing or evidence | ORCA-NTN simulation treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial access** | For KA-SAT, Viasat says an attacker used a VPN appliance misconfiguration to access a trusted management segment. The public incident account does **not** state when that access first occurred. **Dwell before impact: NOT FOUND.** | Give the simulated initial-access event its own timestamp and log record. Any interval from access to first suspicious command is a training parameter, not an incident-derived range. |
| **Dwell / discovery** | Viasat reports lateral movement within the trusted management network. Its public overview does not give a dwell-time range or duration for discovery. **NOT FOUND.** | Use the data-card dwell ranges only as synthetic assumptions. Include both short and longer windows so detectors cannot rely on a fixed delay. |
| **Lateral movement / staging** | Viasat reports reaching a network segment used to manage and operate the network and issuing legitimate commands to many modems. The public source does not provide a complete minute-by-minute staging sequence. | Emit observable management events before the link impact: session context, target count, command type, success and baseline drift. This is a modeling recommendation, not a claim about undisclosed KA-SAT details. |
| **Impact onset** | Viasat observed high-volume malicious traffic from modems at approximately **03:02 UTC on 24 February 2022**. A gradual decline in online modems was observed around the same time. | Model a sequence where telemetry indicates service stress before or alongside widespread terminal loss. Exact synthetic offsets are **ASSUMPTIONS**. |
| **Widespread impact** | Viasat observed larger numbers of modems exiting the network beginning around **04:15 UTC**, over approximately **45 minutes**. It reported that network stabilization began immediately, was largely achieved within hours, and full stabilization took several days. | A KA-SAT-inspired scenario can have a rapid multi-target loss phase and a longer recovery phase. Recovery duration and affected population should be varied; do not present those simulated values as incident measurements. |
| **Telecom signalling disruption example** | In the 2016 Telenor Norway outage, reporting described unusual SS7 signalling preceding an outage affecting around one million customers from approximately **11:30 to 15:00**. The cited report attributed the immediate trigger to rare signalling misinterpreted by Ericsson software; it does not establish malicious intent. | Use it as a fault/abuse ambiguity example, not as proof of an SS7 intrusion. The ORCA-NTN dataset needs signalling-layer fields to model this distinction. [Digi.no report on Telenor outage](https://www.digi.no/telekom/et-ondsinnet-angrep-mot-telenor-ville-hatt-samme-konsekvens/3371835) |

## Suggested scenario parameters

All synthetic ranges below are **ASSUMPTIONS** unless identified as already present in the draft data card. These are training-design ranges, not reported incident measurements. Keep scenarios internally coherent with weather, site criticality, path selection, events and power conditions.

| Scenario | Training range | Harder held-out range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RF jamming-like link degradation** | The data card proposes J/N **3–15 dB**, affecting **1–20 sites**. | The data card proposes J/N **0–3 dB**, affecting **20–50 sites**. | Preserve weak and strong examples; test near the weather/fade boundary. The exact interpretation, calibration and per-link J/N derivation are not defined in the card and should be documented. |
| **Management-plane access and bulk action** | The card proposes attacker dwell **10–60 minutes**. Use **1–5 targets** for some runs and **6–20** for others (**ASSUMPTION**). | The card proposes dwell **2–6 hours**; use a low-and-slow command schedule and **1–5 targets** in some held-out runs (**ASSUMPTION**). | Do not make `wipe` or high target count the only positive signature. Include authorized maintenance twins. |
| **Terminal availability impact** | **1–20 sites** affected; impact over seconds to a few minutes (**ASSUMPTION**). | **20–50 sites**, with staggered or mixed impact over a wider time window (**ASSUMPTION**). | Relate targets to site/gateway dependencies; recovery time is a separate scenario variable. |
| **Festival surge vs service DoS** | Card events (Deepavali, Pongal, payday, Chepauk) with legitimate user/transaction growth. Synthetic DoS-like service degradation may overlap in loss and latency (**ASSUMPTION**). | Use high surge coincident with mild path impairment or partial overload (**ASSUMPTION**). | The model should learn event/service context, not simply label high traffic as malicious. Current data has no flow or queue telemetry. |
| **Fault-weather versus interference** | Existing card rain peak: **20–120 mm/h**. | Existing harder range: **120–180 mm/h**. | These are draft data-card parameters, not official Chennai rainfall statistics. Keep rain-driven `expected_fade_dB` consistent with the chosen weather model. |
| **Fault/power versus tampering** | Existing card battery start range: **40–100%**. | Existing harder range: **15–40%**. | Include grid loss, genset states, flood and site criticality context; avoid labeling power-correlated outages as attack by default. |

**Stealth-level suggestion — ASSUMPTION:** Define stealth by observable behavior, not an attacker procedure: “obvious” has a sharp, broad change; “moderate” has a smaller change and fewer targets; “hard” overlaps benign maintenance, weather or demand while retaining enough ground-truth context for evaluation. Keep the ground-truth labels and scenario seed separate from detector inputs.

## New data-card fields

1. **RF and interference context**
   - Per-link measured and expected fade residual with model version and confidence.
   - Receiver AGC/gain state, carrier lock, modulation/coding changes, power-control command/result, and interference alarm source.
   - Gateway beam, carrier and polarization identity, plus a timestamped interference-report source/severity.
   - Clarify whether `noise_floor_dBm` is measured at terminal or gateway, its bandwidth, and whether values are calibrated across devices.

2. **GNSS/PNT, if included as a detector target**
   - Receiver ID, GNSS fix status, C/N₀ or equivalent signal-quality measure, estimated position/time, clock offset, integrity/alarm flags and receiver-reported interference state.
   - These fields are required to represent jamming/spoofing; link KPIs alone do not establish either behavior.

3. **Management-plane audit context**
   - Session start/end, result/reason, MFA result, source and destination asset IDs, authorization role, session origin category, change-ticket/maintenance-window ID.
   - Per-target command result, target identity, prior/after config hash, firmware/config version and reboot/online result.
   - Approved baselines for user, source, endpoint, login time, command type and typical target count.
   - Avoid retaining real subscriber or operator identifiers in the synthetic data; document pseudonymization.

4. **Telecom signalling, if included as a detector target**
   - Protocol family and generation (SS7/MAP, Diameter, SIP, GTP, 5G service-based interface), source/destination network function category, message family, direction, count/rate, response/result code, and subscriber identifiers in pseudonymous form.
   - Operator/partner trust category, allow-list outcome and anomaly reason.
   - These are needed to distinguish signalling behavior from generic call or data demand. Relevant detection principles are in [ITU-T Q.3066](https://www.itu.int/epublications/publication/itu-t-q-3066-2026-01-principles-for-detection-and-mitigation-of-signalling-attacks-in-telecommunication-networks).

5. **Network, service and power context**
   - Packet/flow counters, queue depth, drops by layer, retransmits, control-plane load, and per-site/per-terminal availability.
   - Maintenance and change windows, fibre/microwave/satellite path fault codes, gateway health and topology dependency IDs.
   - Site-level and terminal-level clock synchronization/telemetry freshness indicators, so missing data can be distinguished from a measured zero.

6. **Labels and provenance**
   - Add label provenance (`measured`, `simulated`, `inferred`), confidence, source scenario, affected asset IDs, earliest observable onset and recovery time.
   - Store technique mapping separately from ground-truth class. One scenario may map to multiple framework techniques; a technique label does not prove malicious intent.
   - Specify all time zones and sampling/aggregation rules. The card currently mixes 10-second, 1-minute and per-event tables.

## Open questions

- Is `jamming J/N` a per-link measured ratio, a simulator control, or a derived label? What receiver bandwidth and link direction does it represent?
- Are `carrier_CN_dB` and `interference_report` measured per carrier, beam or gateway, and can a single event be mapped to affected links?
- Do management logs record per-target results, session duration, failed authentication, MFA, and source authorization? Those are needed to distinguish a maintenance batch from misuse.
- Is the intent to detect GNSS interference or SS7/Diameter abuse? If yes, the current tables cannot train direct detectors for those behaviors; the added receiver and signalling fields are necessary.
- Which terminal/site relationships, gateway dependencies and failover paths are simulated? The model needs this topology to distinguish a shared upstream fault from multiple targeted sites.
- What are the validation and label-generation rules for “attack-jamming” versus “fault-weather” when interference overlaps rain fade, sun outage or equipment faults?
- What evidence supports the draft rain and jamming ranges for the Chennai emulation? The data card already marks its values as physics, calibrated or assumed; retain that provenance at the parameter and event level.
